High-altitude airships are an unusual flight dynamics problem: most of their lift comes from buoyancy rather than from aerodynamic surfaces, and they operate in the thin air of the upper atmosphere. Before any controller can be designed for such a vehicle, it needs a model that captures how it actually moves.

During my internship at CSIR–NAL, I developed a nonlinear six-degree-of-freedom mathematical model of a high-altitude airship in MATLAB/Simulink. Using this model, I identified the airship's trim conditions and studied its open-loop response, characterising the different stability modes of the vehicle.
